Why do Aussie bookies follow the American market?

What does the gap in an alert mean?

It shows how much higher the Aussie price is than the US reference price: the US market's price with the bookmakers' margin removed. For example, if the US reference price has moved to 1.65 and an Aussie bookmaker still offers 1.80, the gap is +9.1% (1.80 ÷ 1.65 − 1). The bigger the gap, the further the Aussie price is behind the US market. For Betfair, an exchange, we use the price after its commission on winnings (6%), so it's a little lower than the price on Betfair's own screen. It's a price difference, not a profit: prices move fast, and you can still lose.

How do you work out the US reference price?

From the live prices at 8 major US sportsbooks. We take each book's margin out of its own prices, then use the median across the books on the same line, with a small correction for NFL player props, where US books price overs a little too short. An alert needs at least 3 US books on that line. If the US line is different from the Aussie one, the price at the Aussie line is estimated with a statistical model, starting from a US line at least 3 books quote. An alert needs the Aussie price to be at least 12% above the US reference price (8% for unders), and the Aussie price is re-checked just before the alert goes out. Read our method in full.

It started with the wind

Odds Ace Australia didn't start in a boardroom. It started with an AFLW game and the wind.

Our founder, Tony Chalhoub, got into the habit of checking the weather before AFLW games, especially the wind. Knowing it was windy wasn't enough: the direction mattered. Was it blowing across the ground, or from one end to the other? How would that change the way the game was played? And had the betting market allowed for it in the price?

That habit led to a much bigger question: what information is out there before it's fully reflected in the price?

From AFLW to the US market

Over years of betting, Tony became more and more focused on how information, timing and price fit together. Weather is only one part of it: injuries, team changes, scheduling, travel and money moving the market all change how a game is priced.

Finding a price is only half of it, too. Bookmakers react quickly, move their prices and can limit accounts, so knowing where a price is available and how fast it's likely to move matters just as much.

Then came American sports. We kept seeing the US market move while prices in Australia hadn't caught up yet: the same idea as the AFLW wind, on a much bigger scale. Information changes. Markets react. But they don't always react at the same time. That's what Odds Ace Australia is built on.

Our method

Method as at 6 October 2026

Every alert compares an Aussie bookmaker's price with the US reference price (margin removed): the chance the US sportsbooks' own prices give once their margin is taken out, turned back into a price. This page sets out exactly how we get it, and when an alert goes out.

1. Live prices from 8 US sportsbooks

Around the clock, every scan reads the current prices at 8 major US sportsbooks for the same game and market. We don't name them, because they aren't licensed in Australia. Game markets (head to head, line and total) usually have most of the 8 quoting. Player props often have fewer. Since 6 October 2026, an alert needs at least 3 US books on the line its price comes from.

On the Aussie side we check 7 Australian-licensed bookmakers: TAB, Sportsbet, Neds, PointsBet, Unibet, Betr and Betfair.

2. Margin out, then the median

Each US book's two prices (over and under, or each team) are turned into chances that add up to 100%, which takes that book's margin out. We use the median of those chances across the books on the same line, never one book's best price, and there must be at least 3 books, so one book out of step can't set the price. The US reference price is 1 divided by that chance.

3. A correction for NFL overs

US books price the over a little too short on NFL yards, receptions and attempts props, so on most of those we shift the chance slightly towards the under.

4. Goal and touchdown scorers

On goal and touchdown scorer markets (anytime goal in the NHL; anytime, first and last touchdown in the NFL), US books only offer "yes", so there's no other side to take the margin from. We take the median US price, from at least 3 books, and remove an estimated margin: 15% for anytime touchdowns, 22% for anytime goals and 30% for first and last touchdowns. These reference prices are rougher than the rest.

5. When the lines differ

If the US line is different from the Aussie one (say 40.5 receiving yards in the US and 45.5 here), the price at the Aussie line is estimated with a statistical model of the player's stat, starting from the US line most books quote, which needs at least 3 books on it. Game lines on a different number are estimated from the US line too. These are estimates, not US prices.

6. The gap

The gap is how much higher the Aussie price is than the US reference price: the Aussie price ÷ the US reference price − 1. For example, an Aussie price of 1.80 against a US reference price of 1.65 is a gap of +9.1%. For Betfair, an exchange, we use the price after its 6% commission on winnings.

7. The bar for an alert

An alert goes out when the gap is at least 12% (8% for unders). From 60 to 30 minutes before the start (the golden window), game lines can alert from 5%, but only if the US market has moved and the Aussie price hasn't.

8. Close to the start

In the last 30 minutes before a game, the gap has to clear the bar against the most cautious US price instead of the median. No new alerts go out in the final 10 minutes.

9. Re-checked before it's sent

Before an alert is posted, the Aussie price is loaded again, usually within a couple of minutes, and the alert only goes out if the gap still clears the bar. On the rare occasion the bookmaker's page won't load a second time, it goes out at the price first found.

10. Sharp-money alerts

A second, rarer kind of alert, marked SHARP MONEY: US betting figures show big money on one side, and an Aussie bookmaker still offers that side at or near the US reference price. These don't need the 12% gap, but they do need at least 3 US books.

What the numbers mean

  • A reference price is an estimate of the US market at that moment, not a prediction of the result. Prices move fast, and any bet can lose.
  • Every alert's Aussie price, US reference price and gap are recorded when it's sent. After the game it's settled from official box scores and added to our results, wins and losses alike.
  • The 12% and 8% bars have applied since 1 October 2026. The first alerts, on 29 and 30 September, used a lower bar.
  • The 3-book minimum has applied since 6 October 2026. Earlier alerts in the results include some that rested on 1 or 2 US books (about a fifth of them).
  • Weather flags for open-air NFL and MLB games are separate posts about wind and rain, with no prices in them.
  • When a rule changes, this page changes with it, and the date at the top says when.
